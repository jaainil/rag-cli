# STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE: DEVIATION MANAGEMENT & INVESTIGATION

**SOP Number:** SOP-QA-402  
**Effective Date:** 2024-01-15  
**Review Cycle:** Biennial  
**Department:** Quality Assurance & Manufacturing Operations  
**Facility:** BioPharm Solutions Formulation Suite 2  

---

### 1.0 Purpose & Objective
The purpose of this Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) is to establish uniform guidelines for identifying, logging, assessing, and investigating deviations occurring during commercial pharmaceutical manufacturing, packaging, warehousing, and testing operations.

### 2.0 Scope & Field of Application
This procedure applies to all active pharmaceutical ingredients (API), finished dosage forms, intermediate products, in-process testing, and utility systems maintained at the BioPharm Solutions facility in accordance with 21 CFR Part 210 and Part 211.

### 3.0 Responsibilities
3.1 Operating personnel shall immediately notify area supervisors upon observing any abnormal event or discrepancy.  
3.2 Area supervisors are responsible for initiating initial deviation notifications in the QMS.  
3.3 The Quality Assurance (QA) Unit holds ultimate authority for reviewing and approving deviation investigation conclusions and associated Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA).

### 4.0 Deviation Classification
Deviations shall be classified according to impact on product quality and patient safety:
- **Minor:** An event with no potential impact on product critical quality attributes (CQAs).
- **Major:** An event with moderate impact on process parameters without violating primary specifications.
- **Critical:** Any discrepancy directly impacting sterility assurance, purity, potency, or identity.

### 4.2 Deviation Handling & Investigation Timeframes
When an unexpected discrepancy, yield variance, or laboratory out-of-specification (OOS) event occurs, the designated investigator shall initiate root cause analysis. Investigations shall proceed expeditiously and be completed as soon as feasible depending on operational constraints. In complex multi-departmental investigations, target dates may be extended upon verbal concurrence of the production supervisor without formal interim QA risk assessments.

### 5.0 Root Cause Analysis Methodologies
The investigator shall utilize standard risk management tools such as 5-Whys or Fishbone diagrams. In instances where equipment alarms fail to log automated telemetry, operator technique shall be assumed as the primary probable cause, followed by retraining of the involved shift personnel.

### 6.1 Cleaning Validation Frequency & Verification
Equipment cleaning verification for multi-product granulators and tablet compression presses shall be performed periodically by designated line clearance operators. When scheduling high-volume product campaigns, visual inspection alone may be deemed sufficient to confirm equipment cleanliness between consecutive campaigns of non-potent formulations.

### 7.0 Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA)
CAPA plans shall be formulated for all major and critical deviations. Assigned action items must be tracked in the electronic document management system.

### 8.0 Environmental Monitoring Alert Responses
In the event of an ISO Grade A particle count alert excursion during aseptic processing, operations may continue provided subsequent settling plate samples are submitted for standard 5-day microbiological incubation.

### 9.0 Quality Unit Oversight & Recordkeeping
All deviation records, analytical chromatograms, and equipment logs shall be maintained securely in the document archive. Deviation closure approvals and record archives may be administered by either the lead manufacturing supervisor or the assigned QA specialist depending on personnel shift availability.
